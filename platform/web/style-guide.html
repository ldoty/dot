Luke & Amber · House style

Daylight & Evening

One visual system for the household’s pages and documents. Daylight is Luke’s side: bright, mid-century, full of plants and morning light. Evening is Amber’s: layered bohemian color (plum, rust, brass, moss) on a warm linen ground. Both are light; the sites use Daylight, and Evening is there for pages that want more warmth.

Principles

Where your tastes meet

Lit from within

Daylight pages feel like a sunny room. Evening pages feel like a warm, layered room. Neither one is a gray office.

Both

Grown, not built

Leaf shapes, wood tones and greens anchor every palette. Use organic curves for illustration and keep the layout grid straight.

Luke, biophilic

Warm geometry

Mid-century forms: pill buttons, round dots, starbursts, a geometric sans. Clean lines, never sterile.

Luke, mid-century

Layered and warm

Evening adds depth: arches, a soft woven texture on the linen ground, plum and rust, and a serif voice for the big numbers.

Amber, bohemian
Color

Two palettes, one family

Both palettes use the same six color roles. Luke’s versions are clear and saturated, Amber’s are earthy and deep, so a page can switch between them without changing its meaning.

Usage

Do and don’t

Do

  • Build every page from the tokens below so both themes work automatically.
  • Make Daylight the default for planning and numbers. Reach for Evening when a page should feel warmer and more personal.
  • Pick one hero accent per page: sun for highlights, pool for actions.
  • Use real photos of plants, wood and natural light when you need imagery.
  • Set figures in DM Mono in tables and Young Serif when one number is the headline.

Don’t

  • Use every accent on one screen. It reads as a toy, not a room.
  • Put sun-colored text on the Daylight ground. It’s too light to read.
  • Add texture to Daylight. The woven dot ground belongs to Evening only.
  • Use ember or leaf for decoration. They mean bad and good.
  • Swap in other fonts. The four faces cover every need.
